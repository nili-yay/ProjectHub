Pulmonary Veins Segmentation

Pulmonary veins play an essential role in the cardiovascular system by transporting oxygen-rich blood from the lungs to the left atrium of the heart. This project focused on extracting pulmonary vein geometry from CT scan images and analyzing the resulting models using geometric measurements and computational fluid dynamics.

Project Overview

In this project, we studied how to identify and isolate pulmonary veins from CT imaging data. In raw CT scans, vascular structures are not always easy to distinguish, so segmentation is a critical step in converting medical image data into usable 3D anatomical geometry.

The core question behind this work was: how can we define the region of interest clearly enough for software to extract the pulmonary veins accurately?

Geometric Analysis

After segmentation, we computed several important geometric measurements to characterize patient-specific vein structures:

  • Tortuosity: how curved or winding the vein is relative to a straight path
  • Curvature: how the vessel bends in three-dimensional space
  • Radius: an indicator of vessel size and potential blood flow capacity

CFD Simulations

We also performed laminar, steady-state CFD simulations on patient-specific pulmonary vein models to investigate hemodynamic quantities such as:

  • Wall Shear Stress (WSS)
  • Velocity profiles
  • Pressure distribution

Boundary Conditions

Simulations were run using constant velocity inlet boundary conditions, since patient-specific inlet velocity data is not always available directly from CT imaging.

ANSYS Fluent Workflow

Part of the simulation workflow was explored in ANSYS Fluent to better understand the setup, solution process, and interpretation of hemodynamic results in a biomedical context.
